I build metals into oxidation states and bonding motifs they aren't supposed to hold.

My research, conducted in the Fortier Laboratory at UTEP under Prof. Skye Fortier, centers on low-valent and multiply-bonded early transition metal complexes — chemistry that sits outside the conventional stability window for these elements. This includes a titanium chalcogenide series spanning the Ti(IV)/Ti(III) redox couple across E = N, O, S, Se, and Te, masked Zr(II) synthons stabilized against disproportionation, and a rare linear Fe(II) complex supported by imidazolin-2-iminato ligands. Most of what I make cannot survive light, air, or a careless breath of moisture, so it is born and lives entirely on the Schlenk line and behind glovebox glass.

Every new compound is characterized as completely as the chemistry demands: single-crystal X-ray diffraction to confirm structure and bond order, multinuclear NMR — including paramagnetic systems — to probe solution behavior, and EPR for electronic structure.

Outside the glovebox, I write the software that speeds this work up — turning repetitive, time-consuming data processing into something faster and more intuitive. Time in this field is finite and rarely forgiving, and I'd rather spend it on the chemistry.
