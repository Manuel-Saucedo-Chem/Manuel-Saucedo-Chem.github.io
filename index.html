I make molecules, grow crystals, and write the code that helps me understand them.

My work lives in the low-valent chemistry of titanium, zirconium, and iron — building multiple bonds where few expect to find them (Ti=E chalcogenides across E = N, O, S, Se, Te), coaxing zirconium down to Zr(II), and pushing iron into rare linear geometries with imidazolin-2-iminato ligands. Most of what I make doesn't survive contact with air or light, so synthesis happens on the Schlenk line and in the glovebox, and every compound gets the full workup afterward: X-ray diffraction to see the structure, multinuclear NMR and EPR (paramagnetic complexes included) to read the electronics, and beamtime at NSLS-II for X-ray absorption spectroscopy.

I got here by way of electrochemistry — my undergrad research was electroanalytical methods, which turned into two publications before I ever touched a glovebox. That background still shapes how I work: I run cyclic voltammetry alongside crystallography on new compounds, and I built a Python-based CV analysis suite to keep the data side moving fast.
